# Build the Knowledge Primitive First — The Cards Are the Product, Every Output Is Just a Rendering

**Source:** Claude Boot Camp planning session, 2026-09-11. Generalizes the method proven on *Geography as Destiny*.
**Type:** preference
**Verified:** ran-it — the book has run this method to 340+ cards, producing an epub, a website, chapters, figures, and an MCP server from one card corpus.
**Relevant to:** general (the method the whole project rests on)

## Content

The actual work is building a **knowledge primitive**: a repeatable way to create, document, and synthesize what you've learned. The boot camp is one output of that primitive. A conference deck, an MCP server, a book, a client workshop are others. All get generated later, from whatever accumulated.

The operative rule, for when the two pull apart:

> If a decision makes the cards better but the boot camp slightly harder to assemble, **make the cards better.**

Format is downstream and cheap. The underlying material is the expensive part.

Corollaries that follow:

- **Don't optimize for an output format up front.** Deciding "this is a six-module course" early distorts what gets captured — you start filing what fits the modules instead of what's true.
- **File as encountered, organize later.** Categorization is a synthesis-time decision, made when the shape is actually known.
- **Work tangent-following, not linear.** Follow what's interesting or useful now. Don't work sections in order, and don't build things for completeness.
- **Not everything deserves a lab.** For products you know well, card what's already in your head. For products you don't, approach as a consultant/architect first — ask what the use cases and real value are, and let that decide whether it's worth implementing at all.

## Why this matters

Most curriculum effort dies because it's organized around the deliverable. You commit to a shape, discover the material doesn't support it, and either pad the weak sections or quietly ship something thin. Because the shape was decided first, there's no reservoir to draw on when a section turns out to need more.

Inverting it — accumulate first, shape later — means the deliverable is assembled from material that already exists, and its structure reflects what you actually learned rather than what you guessed at the start. It also means one corpus yields many outputs, so a new format is an afternoon of assembly rather than a new project.

The book is the existence proof: same cards behind the epub, the web chapters, the figures, and the MCP server.
