# Anthropic Academy Covers the Lecture Register, Not the Hands-On One — Point At It, Don't Rewrite It

**Source:** Dave reviewed [Anthropic Academy](https://anthropic.skilljar.com) during the 2026-09-11 planning session, specifically to check whether this curriculum would be redundant.
**Type:** product-behavior
**Verified:** docs — reviewed the catalog and course structure firsthand; did not complete courses end to end. `[confirm-this: revisit if Academy adds hands-on lab content — that would change the calculus.]`
**Relevant to:** all sections (affects how every lecture beat gets written)

## Content

Anthropic Academy is good, and it is squarely **lecture material**: high-level, conceptual, explains what things are and why they matter. What it is not is hands-on — it doesn't sit you in a terminal and make you hit the failure.

That maps exactly onto one of the three beats in this curriculum's topic format (lecture → lab → takeaway). The lecture beat is, in many cases, **already covered well by someone else.**

The resulting rule:

- Where a topic's lecture is already well covered — Academy, official docs, a good talk — the lecture beat may be a **pointer**: "go read/watch X; here's why it matters and what it means for your situation." The card records `Verified: docs`.
- **Labs stay original.** A lab is by definition something actually run, and that's the half no existing course provides.

## Why this matters

The instinct when building a curriculum is to cover everything, because gaps feel like sloppiness. That instinct produces a worse product: a mediocre re-narration of concepts Anthropic already explains well, which also has to be maintained as those products change.

The value this project adds is the other two beats — the hands-on implementation, and the *judgment about what's worth doing at all*. Those are the scarce parts. Spending effort re-explaining what a context window is takes time away from them.

This is the same "define by what it's NOT" discipline used elsewhere: scope by exclusion, and be explicit that the exclusion is deliberate rather than an oversight.
