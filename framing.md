# Framing

Notes for the eventual front matter — the intro to the boot camp, the book, the deck, whatever this becomes. Captured early so the stance is settled before the prose gets written.

## Why listen to me

A brief personal framing: how I think, and real things I've built. Some were planned, some were serendipitous — and the serendipitous ones matter just as much, because they're evidence of what happens when you follow a thread instead of a roadmap.

The point of showing the work isn't credentials. It's to make the case that you should build, experiment, follow your own curiosity, and solve your own frictions. If someone leaves having built something small for themselves, that's the outcome.

## What this is not

**Not selling anything.** Not the approach, not Claude, not Anthropic. If a tool doesn't fit your situation, don't use it — and the material should say so where it's true.

**Not claiming this is *the* right way.** It's *a* way. Mine. The demo is one path; the takeaway exists so you find yours. This applies at every level — the workflows, the tooling choices, the whole method.

## Stay flexible

These tools and models change constantly. Even minor updates shift the working dynamics — what was awkward last month is now a one-liner, and habits built around a limitation outlive the limitation.

So the material should teach a way of *evaluating and adapting*, not a fixed recipe. Anything written as "always do X" will be wrong within a year. Prefer explaining the underlying constraint, so a reader can tell when it stops applying.

This is also an argument for the card method: cards carry their own timestamps and `Verified` status, so it's visible when a note is getting stale, rather than having stale advice baked invisibly into polished prose.
